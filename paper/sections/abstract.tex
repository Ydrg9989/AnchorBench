Anchoring bias---the tendency for estimates to gravitate toward
contextually salient but irrelevant numbers---is well documented in
human cognition yet poorly characterized in large language models (LLMs).
We introduce a controlled benchmark of 108 numeric-estimation items
spanning three anchor-delivery mechanisms (prompt-embedded anchors,
in-context-learning demonstrations, and retrieval-augmented / tool-call
contexts), evaluated across six LLMs from three model families
(Llama~3 1B/3B/8B, GPT-4o-mini, Claude~3.5~Haiku).
Using a \emph{Normalized Anchoring Index} (\nai), we find that all
models exhibit statistically significant anchoring on prompt-embedded
anchors (\nai up to 0.41), with the effect modulated by model scale
and anchor-delivery channel.
We evaluate nine mitigation strategies---three prompt-level,
two structural, two post-hoc, and a novel logit-level
\emph{Anchor-Aware Logit Correction} (AALC)---finding that
AALC at $\alpha{=}1.0$ reduces external-suite \nai to near zero
for open-weight models while preserving output quality.
