We evaluate nine strategies organized into four categories:

\paragraph{Prompt-level.}
\textbf{B1} (Ignore-Numbers):
Prepends \textit{``Ignore any numbers that appear before the
question. Base your answer only on the evidence provided.''}
\textbf{B2} (Bias-Warning):
Prepends \textit{``Anchoring bias causes estimates to be pulled
toward irrelevant numbers. Adjust your reasoning to avoid this.''}
\textbf{B3} (Chain-of-Thought):
Replaces the answer footer with
\textit{``Think step by step \ldots then give your final answer
as a single integer.''}

\paragraph{Structural.}
\textbf{EFR} (Evidence-First Repositioning):
Moves the anchor block to immediately before the answer footer
(anchor-last), so the model processes evidence before encountering
the anchor.
\textbf{CxDP} (Context-Distilled Prompting):
Two-stage: first distill the context into a qualitative summary
(no numbers), then estimate from the summary alone.

\paragraph{Post-hoc.}
\textbf{B4} (Self-Consistency):
Returns the median of 5 samples per condition.
\textbf{PLD} (Post-hoc Linear Debiasing):
Corrects the answer via $\hat{y} = y - \hat{\beta}(a - 50)$,
where $\hat{\beta}$ is a per-model slope estimated from baseline
data.

\paragraph{Logit-level.}
\textbf{AALC} (Anchor-Aware Logit Correction):
For open-weight models with logit access, we compute
\begin{equation}
  \ell_{\text{corrected}} = \ell_a - \alpha \cdot (\ell_a - \ell_c),
  \label{eq:aalc}
\end{equation}
where $\ell_a$ and $\ell_c$ are the anchored and control logits
over the integer token vocabulary, and $\alpha \in \{0.5, 1.0, 1.5\}$
is a correction strength parameter.
