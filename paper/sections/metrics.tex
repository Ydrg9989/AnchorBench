All suites share a single evaluation framework.
The unified metrics enable direct cross-suite comparison of
anchoring susceptibility, regardless of how anchors reach the
model.

\paragraph{Task accuracy (control-only).}
We report mean absolute error
$\text{MAE}_{\text{ctrl}} =
  \frac{1}{n}\sum_i |y_{\text{ctrl},i} -
  y^{*}_{\text{evidence},i}|$
and accuracy-within-$\pm 10$
$\text{Acc}_{10} =
  \frac{1}{n}\sum_i \mathbf{1}[|y_{\text{ctrl},i} -
  y^{*}_{\text{evidence},i}| \leq 10]$,
computed exclusively on \texttt{control}-condition responses.
Restricting to control ensures that task accuracy measures
pure estimation ability without any anchor influence.

\paragraph{Unified Anchor Influence (UAI).}
For each item~$i$ under anchored condition~$r$ with anchor
value~$a_{i,r}$, we define:
\begin{equation}
  \text{UAI}_{i,r} =
    \frac{y_{\text{anchor},i,r} - y_{\text{ctrl},i}}
         {a_{i,r} - y_{\text{ctrl},i}},
  \label{eq:uai}
\end{equation}
where $y_{\text{anchor},i,r}$ is the model's response under
the anchored condition and $y_{\text{ctrl},i}$ is its
matched control response for the same item.
$\text{UAI} = 1$ indicates that the model's answer shifts by
the full anchor--control gap (complete susceptibility);
$\text{UAI} = 0$ indicates no anchoring.
Items where $|a_{i,r} - y_{\text{ctrl},i}| < \varepsilon$
are excluded ($\varepsilon = 3$) to avoid division by small
denominators.
Separate means are computed for irrelevant
($\text{UAI}_{\text{irr}}$) and plausible
($\text{UAI}_{\text{plaus}}$) conditions, each averaging
over both low- and high-anchor items.

The UAI denominates by the anchor--control distance rather
than the anchor--gold distance used in some prior work.
This choice makes the metric interpretable as the fraction of
the model's available shift (from its own baseline toward the
anchor) that is actually realized, and avoids conflating
anchoring magnitude with task-accuracy errors in the control
condition.

\paragraph{Anchor-value mapping.}
For the External, ICL, RAG, and Tool suites, $a_{i,r}$ is the
experimenter-defined anchor value ($\theta \pm \delta$, where
$\delta \in \{15, 25, 40\}$).
For the History suite, $a_{i,r}$ is the model's own
Stage~1 answer, which serves as the self-generated anchor.
This mapping unifies all five paradigms under a single
formula while preserving the paradigm-specific semantics of
what constitutes the anchor.

\paragraph{Toward-Anchor Rate (TAR).}
The binary toward-anchor rate measures the fraction of items
where the model's response moves in the direction of the
anchor relative to its control baseline:
\begin{equation}
  \text{TAR}_{i,r} = \mathbf{1}\big[
    (y_{\text{anchor},i,r} - y_{\text{ctrl},i}) \cdot
    (a_{i,r} - y_{\text{ctrl},i}) > 0
  \big].
  \label{eq:tar}
\end{equation}
$\text{TAR} = 0.50$ indicates chance-level directional
influence; $\text{TAR} > 0.50$ indicates systematic shift
toward anchors.
Unlike UAI, TAR does not require epsilon filtering and
captures even small directional effects.

\paragraph{Discrimination gap.}
The discrimination gap quantifies whether a model
differentiates between irrelevant and plausible anchors:
\begin{equation}
  \text{Disc}_{\Delta} =
    \text{UAI}_{\text{plaus}} - \text{UAI}_{\text{irr}}.
  \label{eq:disc}
\end{equation}
$\text{Disc}_{\Delta} > 0$ indicates greater sensitivity to
plausible than irrelevant anchors (the desired pattern);
$\text{Disc}_{\Delta} \approx 0$ indicates indiscriminate
anchoring;
$\text{Disc}_{\Delta} < 0$ indicates greater sensitivity to
irrelevant anchors---the worst-case failure mode.
Unlike a ratio-based formulation, the difference is
well-defined even when $\text{UAI}_{\text{plaus}} \approx 0$.

\paragraph{History-suite secondary metrics.}
The History suite's two-stage protocol additionally supports
two adjustment-specific metrics reported in
Appendix~\ref{app:history-secondary}:
the \emph{Adjustment Completion Ratio}
($\text{ACR} = (y^{(2)} - y^{(1)}) /
(y^{*}_{\text{evidence}} - y^{(1)})$), measuring how
completely the model corrects from Stage~1 to Stage~2,
and the \emph{Residual Reduction}
($\text{RR} = 1 - |y^{(2)} - y^{*}| / |y^{(1)} - y^{*}|$),
measuring whether Stage~2 reduces error.
These metrics complement the unified framework by capturing
adjustment dynamics unique to the self-generated paradigm.

\paragraph{Parse rate.}
The fraction of prompts for which an integer in $[0, 100]$ was
successfully extracted from the model's response, using a
regex-first pipeline with an optional LLM fallback
(\S\ref{sec:results}).
