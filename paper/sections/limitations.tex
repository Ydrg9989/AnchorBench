\paragraph{Suite coverage.}
The current experimental validation covers all five suites
(External, History, ICL, RAG, and Tool) across four models.
The five-suite pilots demonstrate rich paradigm-dependent
anchoring patterns---including a model-family accuracy split
unique to tool outputs, cross-suite discrimination reversals,
and the ICL suite's confirmation that metadata priming is the
subtlest anchoring pathway.

\paragraph{History suite: control structure.}
The History suite uses a single-stage control but a two-stage
protocol for anchored conditions.
This structural asymmetry means that observed differences could
partly reflect the multi-turn format rather than anchoring
alone.
A two-stage control (with neutral Stage~1 content) would
address this confound but would require careful design to
avoid introducing its own priming effects.

\paragraph{ICL suite design.}
The ICL suite embeds irrelevant metadata numbers in
demonstration context while keeping demo answers neutral.
An alternative design using biased demo answers would test
ICL distribution-matching rather than clean numeric priming;
we chose the metadata-number design to isolate the priming
mechanism, but acknowledge that the biased-answer variant
may capture a complementary failure mode.

\paragraph{Tool suite: confounds and ecological validity.}
The primary Tool-suite evaluation uses injected tool outputs
(the full tool-call exchange is pre-constructed in the prompt
context).
An ecological validation subset confirms that results
generalize to genuine multi-turn function-calling interaction,
but the ecological subset is small (${\sim}50$~items,
control~+~plausible\_high only) and serves as a sanity check
rather than a comprehensive evaluation.
Additionally, the large accuracy and parse-rate gap between
model families on the Tool suite means that anchoring
susceptibility and structured-context competence are
confounded: models that struggle with tool-output formatting
may also show inflated anchoring metrics.
Disentangling these factors would require models with
comparable tool-context competence or explicit controls for
formatting ability.

\paragraph{Model coverage.}
The pilot evaluates four open-weight models from two families
(Qwen, Llama) at 3B and 7--8B scale.
Larger models, API-served models (GPT, Claude, Gemini), and
models with explicit reasoning capabilities remain untested.
We do not claim that the patterns observed in the pilot
generalize across all model families and scales.

\paragraph{Single language.}
All items are in English.
Anchoring susceptibility may vary across languages,
particularly for models with different language training
distributions.

\paragraph{Synthetic anchors.}
Benchmark anchors are constructed rather than naturally
occurring.
Real-world anchors encountered in deployed systems may differ
in salience and framing.

\paragraph{Future directions.}
The benchmark is designed to support several extensions beyond
its current scope:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Extended-scale evaluation} across all five suites
  with larger item counts and the difficulty axis, enabling
  finer-grained cross-paradigm comparisons.
  The current pilot already reveals that anchoring
  susceptibility is paradigm-dependent; larger samples
  will enable per-domain and per-difficulty analyses.
\item \textbf{Mitigation evaluation:} the benchmark can serve
  as a controlled testbed for inference-time debiasing
  strategies, measuring whether mitigations reduce irrelevant
  anchoring without suppressing appropriate sensitivity to
  informative context.
\item \textbf{Mechanistic analysis:} the paired-condition
  design (identical prompts differing only in the anchor
  component) supports activation patching and logit-lens
  analyses to localize where in the transformer architecture
  anchoring arises.
  The History suite's two-stage protocol additionally enables
  comparison of internal representations between Stage~1 and
  Stage~2 to study how prior estimates persist across turns.
\item \textbf{Informative-anchor validation:} extending the
  relevance axis to include genuinely informative anchors,
  testing whether models appropriately increase sensitivity
  when the anchor is evidentially justified.
\end{itemize}
