% ── Paragraph 1: Why anchoring matters as a reliability issue ──
When LLMs are deployed for numeric estimation in high-stakes
domains---medical triage scores, financial risk indices,
operational efficiency ratings---their outputs must be robust
to irrelevant contextual numbers.
Anchoring, the systematic tendency to shift estimates toward
salient reference points~\citep{tversky1974judgment}, is among
the most robust biases documented in human
judgment~\citep{kahneman2011thinking, chapman1999anchoring}.
Recent work has confirmed that LLMs exhibit analogous behavior:
model estimates shift toward numbers embedded in prompts,
conversation history, and retrieved
context~\citep{jones2022capturing, hagendorff2023humanlike,
echterhoff-etal-2024-cognitive, huang2025anchoring,
lou2024anchoring}.
Unlike generic accuracy degradation, anchoring is particularly
insidious because biased outputs can appear confident and
well-reasoned, making the failure mode difficult to detect
without controlled evaluation.

% ── Paragraph 2: Why current evidence is insufficient ──────────
However, existing LLM anchoring evaluations are fragmented and
insufficient for systematic benchmarking.
Most studies test only prompt-embedded anchors, ignoring the
multiple interfaces through which anchors reach real LLM
systems---conversation history, few-shot demonstrations,
retrieved documents, and tool outputs.
More fundamentally, current evaluations treat \emph{all} numeric
sensitivity as uniformly undesirable, conflating irrational
susceptibility to irrelevant numbers with potentially rational
use of informative context.
A model that appropriately adjusts its estimate given a credible
prior is behaving differently from one that shifts toward an
arbitrary case~ID---yet existing metrics cannot distinguish
these behaviors.
Finally, few studies ground their designs in the cognitive
science literature, which identifies multiple distinct anchoring
mechanisms~\citep{epley2001putting, mussweiler1999hypothesis,
chapman1999anchoring} that may map onto different LLM failure
modes.
In short, prior work establishes \emph{that} LLMs can anchor
but leaves open \emph{when} and \emph{through which interface}
anchoring arises, and whether sensitivity tracks the
normative relevance of the anchor.

% ── Paragraph 3: Our benchmark idea and theory grounding ───────
We introduce \textsc{AnchorBench}, a benchmark that addresses
these limitations through two key design choices.
First, we map four established human anchoring paradigms---classic
external anchoring~\citep{tversky1974judgment}, self-generated
anchoring via insufficient
adjustment~\citep{epley2001putting}, numeric priming through
selective accessibility~\citep{mussweiler1999hypothesis}, and
ecological anchoring in realistic information environments---to
five LLM interface channels (prompt-embedded, conversation
history, in-context learning, retrieval-augmented generation,
and tool-call outputs).
Second, we introduce an explicit \emph{anchor-relevance axis}
with four levels (none, irrelevant, plausible, informative)
that operationalizes the normative status of each anchor.
This axis enables the benchmark to measure not just how much a
model's estimates shift, but whether the model discriminates
between anchors it should ignore and anchors that carry genuine
evidential weight.

% ── Paragraph 4: Contributions ─────────────────────────────────
Our contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Theory-grounded benchmark design.}
  A systematic mapping from four human anchoring paradigms to
  five LLM interface channels, with an anchor-relevance axis
  that separates susceptibility from discrimination
  (\S\ref{sec:dataset}).

\item \textbf{Evidence-first task construction.}
  Each benchmark item presents structured numeric evidence
  from which a gold answer
  ($y^{*}_{\text{evidence}}$) is derivable, ensuring that
  anchoring is measured against a well-defined rational
  baseline (\S\ref{sec:gold-rule}).

\item \textbf{Unified evaluation framework.}
  A single set of metrics---Unified Anchor Influence (UAI),
  Toward-Anchor Rate (TAR), and discrimination gap
  (Disc$_{\Delta}$)---that enables direct comparison across
  suites with different anchor-delivery mechanisms, replacing
  suite-specific metrics with a coherent framework
  (\S\ref{sec:metrics}).

\item \textbf{Five-suite pilot validation.}
  Pilot studies on the external suite (90~items, 4~models),
  the history suite (60~items, two-stage self-generated
  protocol), the ICL suite (180~items, metadata priming in
  few-shot demonstrations), the RAG suite (180~items,
  three-document corpus design), and the tool suite
  (180~items, structured tool outputs via chat template)
  demonstrate that anchoring susceptibility is
  paradigm-dependent: model rankings on both accuracy
  and discrimination reverse across suites, and plausible
  anchors produce stronger influence than irrelevant ones in
  18 of 20 model--suite combinations
  (\S\ref{sec:results}).
\end{enumerate}

\noindent We release the benchmark, generation pipeline, and
evaluation code to support reproducible research on LLM
anchoring.
