We complement the behavioral analysis with a proof-of-concept
mechanistic investigation that asks \emph{where} in the transformer
anchoring distortion arises and whether it can be localized to
specific depth regions.  All analyses use teacher-forced scoring
(no sampling) on the external suite ($n{=}30$ items), applied to
the three open-weight Llama models.

\subsection{Methods}

\paragraph{Anchor imprint.}
For each item we compute the integer output distribution
$P(y)$ for $y \in \{0,\ldots,100\}$ under the control, low-anchor,
and high-anchor prompts by extracting logits at the last token
position and restricting to the single-token integer vocabulary.
We measure the distributional shift via
KL$(P_{\text{anchor}} \| P_{\text{control}})$,
Total Variation Distance (TVD), Wasserstein-1 ($W_1$), and the
expected-value shift
$\Delta\text{EV} = \mathbb{E}[Y_{\text{anchor}}]
  - \mathbb{E}[Y_{\text{control}}]$.
The EV shift is the distributional counterpart of the behavioral
\nai: $\text{NAI} = (\Delta\text{EV}_{\text{high}}
  - \Delta\text{EV}_{\text{low}}) / 60$.

\paragraph{Logit lens.}
At each transformer layer $l$, we apply the final layer norm and
language-model head to the residual stream at the last token
position, yielding a layer-wise integer distribution $P_l(y)$.
We compute JS$(P_l^{\text{anchor}} \| P_l^{\text{control}})$ at
every layer to identify the depth at which the anchor begins
to distort the output distribution.

\paragraph{Residual-stream patching.}
We perform coarse causal attribution via activation patching:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textit{Clean run:} forward the control prompt, caching
  hidden states at every layer.
\item \textit{Patched run:} forward the anchored prompt with
  hooks that replace the residual stream at the last token
  position for a contiguous bucket of layers with the cached
  clean states.
\end{enumerate}
Layers are divided into three equal-sized buckets (early, mid,
late).  The \emph{restoration} metric quantifies how much of the
anchor-induced divergence is removed:
\begin{equation}
  \text{rest}(b) = 1 -
    \frac{D_{\text{JS}}(\hat{P}^b, P_{\text{ctrl}})}
         {D_{\text{JS}}(P_{\text{anch}}, P_{\text{ctrl}})},
  \label{eq:restoration}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{P}^b$ is the output distribution after patching
bucket $b$.  A restoration of 1 means the patch fully removes
the anchoring distortion.

\subsection{Logit-lens localization}

Figure~\ref{fig:logit_lens} shows the layer-wise
JS divergence between anchored and control distributions.

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{logit_lens_combined.pdf}
\caption{Logit lens: JS divergence between anchored and control
integer distributions as a function of relative depth
(layer~$l$ / total layers~$L$).
Shaded regions show 95\% CIs across items.
In all three models, divergence is near zero in early layers
and rises sharply in the final third, indicating that the
anchor distortion is concentrated in late layers.}
\label{fig:logit_lens}
\end{figure}

Across all three models, the anchor--control divergence remains
near zero in early layers and rises sharply in the final third
of the network.  This ``late-onset'' pattern suggests that the
anchor does not corrupt early representations; rather, the
distortion is introduced (or amplified) in the later layers
that are most directly responsible for mapping representations
to output tokens.

The causal attribution results are striking in their consistency:
patching the late third of layers restores ${\geq}98\%$ of the
anchoring divergence across all three model sizes.  Early-layer
patching has negligible effect (${\leq}7\%$).  Mid-layer
restoration is moderate for the 3B and 8B models (0.43 and 0.38)
but noisy for the 1B model ($-$0.16 $\pm$ 0.67), suggesting that
the 1B model's anchoring mechanism is more distributed across the
mid--late boundary.

These results are consistent with the logit-lens findings and
jointly suggest that anchoring distortion in these models is a
\emph{late-layer} phenomenon: the anchor token is processed
through the full context, but the actual shift in the integer
output distribution occurs predominantly in the layers closest
to the output head.

\subsection{Limitations of the mechanistic analysis}

\paragraph{External suite only.}
The patching design requires paired control/anchor prompts of
similar structure, which is cleanly available only for the
external suite.  ICL, RAG, and tool suites involve structural
prompt differences that confound patching.

\paragraph{Coarse granularity.}
We partition layers into three buckets for robustness across
model sizes.  This cannot pinpoint individual layers, attention
heads, or MLP sublayers.  Finer-grained causal tracing
(\eg per-layer, per-head) is left for future work.

\paragraph{Single-token approximation.}
The logit-lens and patching analyses score only single-token
integers.  Multi-token answers (e.g., ``100'') are handled
correctly in the imprint analysis but not in the layer-wise
diagnostics.

\paragraph{No attention-head attribution.}
We do not perform head-level ablation or probing.  Identifying
specific ``anchor heads'' or circuits requires more targeted
interventions.
