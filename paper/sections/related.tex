\paragraph{Cognitive biases in LLMs.}
Several studies have examined whether LLMs exhibit human-like
cognitive biases.  \citet{jones2022capturing} proposed a taxonomy
mapping human heuristics to LLM failure modes.
\citet{hagendorff2023cognitive} found that GPT-family models
exhibited anchoring and framing effects that diminished with
instruction tuning.  \citet{agrawal2024cognitive} systematically
tested 14 biases across multiple LLMs.

\paragraph{Anchoring in LLMs.}
\citet{itzhak2024anchored} distinguished between \emph{instructed}
and \emph{default} anchoring, showing that models differ in
susceptibility.  The \texttt{tum-nlp/cognitive-biases-in-llms}
dataset~\citep{tumnlp2024biases} provides control/treatment pairs
for multiple biases including anchoring, but uses ordinal scales
(1--9) rather than continuous numeric estimates.

\paragraph{Mitigation.}
Chain-of-thought prompting~\citep{wei2022cot} and
self-consistency~\citep{wang2023selfconsistency} are general
strategies that may reduce bias.  Contrastive decoding methods such
as DoLa~\citep{chuang2024dola} operate at the logit level.
To our knowledge, no prior work has proposed a logit-level
correction specifically targeting numeric anchoring.
