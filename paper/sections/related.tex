% ── Human anchoring theory ─────────────────────────────────────
\paragraph{Human anchoring theory.}
Anchoring was first identified by \citet{tversky1974judgment} as
one of three judgment heuristics, demonstrated through the
classic experiment in which randomly generated numbers influenced
estimates of factual quantities.
Subsequent research revealed that anchoring is not a unitary
phenomenon.
\citet{epley2001putting} distinguished \emph{self-generated}
anchoring, where individuals start from a self-produced value
and adjust insufficiently, from \emph{experimenter-provided}
anchoring, which operates through different cognitive pathways.
\citet{mussweiler1999hypothesis} proposed the \emph{selective
accessibility} model, in which anchors prime anchor-consistent
knowledge via hypothesis-consistent testing, operating even when
the anchor is transparently uninformative.
\citet{chapman1999anchoring} reviewed anchoring in applied
judgment, identifying conditions under which it is amplified
(uncertainty, cognitive load) or attenuated (expertise,
incentives).
A key lesson from this literature is that different paradigms
produce anchoring through distinct mechanisms, and that rational
agents should respond differently to informative versus
uninformative reference points---a distinction that motivates
the relevance axis in our benchmark.

% ── LLM anchoring studies ─────────────────────────────────────
\paragraph{Anchoring and cognitive biases in LLMs.}
Several studies have documented anchoring-like behavior in LLMs.
\citet{jones2022capturing} mapped human cognitive biases to LLM
failure modes, proposing a taxonomy that includes anchoring.
\citet{hagendorff2023humanlike} found that GPT-family models
exhibited anchoring and framing effects that diminished with
instruction tuning.
\citet{echterhoff-etal-2024-cognitive} systematically tested
14~cognitive biases across multiple LLMs.
\citet{itzhak2023instructed} distinguished instructed and
default anchoring, showing that models vary in susceptibility.
More recently, \citet{lou2024anchoring} tested anchoring across
GPT-4 and Gemini with multiple mitigation strategies,
\citet{huang2025anchoring} studied anchoring mechanisms and
mitigations using synthetic anchors,
\citet{takenami2025anchoring} demonstrated anchoring effects in
LLM-driven price negotiation simulations,
and \citet{valencia2025anchors} combined behavioral analysis
with Shapley-value attribution to measure anchor influence on
output distributions.
The \texttt{tum-nlp} cognitive bias
dataset~\citep{malberg-etal-2025-comprehensive} provides
control/treatment pairs for multiple biases but uses ordinal
Likert scales rather than continuous numeric estimates.
While these studies collectively establish that LLMs exhibit
anchoring, they share common limitations: most test only one
or two delivery mechanisms (typically prompt-embedded), none
introduces a relevance-control axis, and none provides a
systematic mapping to the cognitive science paradigms that
distinguish anchoring mechanisms.

% ── Bias benchmarks and evaluator bias ────────────────────────
\paragraph{Bias benchmarks and evaluator bias.}
Beyond anchoring-specific work, the broader LLM evaluation
literature highlights the importance of controlled bias
measurement.
\citet{koo2024benchmarking} showed that LLMs acting as
evaluators exhibit systematic cognitive biases, suggesting that
anchoring effects extend beyond estimation tasks to evaluation
settings.
Bias benchmarks for social biases have established the value of
controlled, paired evaluation designs, but no analogous
theory-grounded benchmark exists for cognitive biases in numeric
estimation.
Our work fills this gap by providing a benchmark specifically
designed to separate susceptibility from relevance
discrimination---a distinction that, to our knowledge, no
existing LLM anchoring evaluation operationalizes.
